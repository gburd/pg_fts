# 5-way BM25 comparison on pg_fts 1.5.9 (2026-09-06)

Re-run of the identical-single-column methodology from `RESULTS_5WAY_159` against
the just-released **1.5.9** (block-granular doclen decode + shared resident
block). Every engine indexes **byte-identical text**: the corpus TSV is
pre-joined to exactly two columns (`id`, `content` = Wikipedia title + ' ' +
body), each engine loads `docs(id bigint, content text)`, and each builds its
index on `content` and nothing else. 2,188,038 rows on every host, verified.

## Rig
EC2 **r6id.4xlarge** (account `bene`), 16 vCPU, 128GB, 884GB local NVMe instance
store, Amazon Linux 2023, PostgreSQL **17.10** built from source,
`shared_buffers=64GB`. One dedicated instance per engine. Warm `\timing`.

**Methodology correction vs the previous run:** 8 runs per query, **median of the
last 5**. The previous run dropped only one cold run, which was NOT enough for
pg_search and vchord — both need 2-3 runs to reach steady state. This mattered:
the pg_search agent reported 6.86 ms for rare k10, but runs 4-8 settle at
**2.13 ms** (3× error). All four arms were re-measured by hand under the 8-run
rule; two agent-reported arms were corrected, two confirmed.

Versions: pg_fts **1.5.9** (`56f5328`, extversion confirmed 1.5.9, .so mtime
checked); pg_textsearch `f940210`; pg_search paradedb `c807ede` / pg_search
0.25.6 / pgrx 0.19.2 / pgvector 0.8.5; VectorChord-bm25 `14fc2a3` / pgrx 0.17.0.

## Match counts — three of four engines still agree exactly

|----------|-------------|-------------|---------------|-------------|
| slovakia | 10,875      | 10,875      | 10,875        | 10,853      |
| hungary  | 24,097      | 24,097      | 24,097        | 23,990      |
| **year** | **734,896** | **734,896** | **734,896**   | **495,580** |

Unchanged from the previous run and independently re-derived. pg_fts, vchord
(`to_tsvector('english', …)`) and pg_textsearch (`text_config='english'`) all use
PostgreSQL's `english` config and agree byte-for-byte. **pg_search is 33% low on
`year` because Tantivy does not stem** — regex ground truth on the corpus:
`\myear\M` = 501,231, `\myears\M` = 461,682, `\myears?\M` = **733,960**. So
pg_fts scans ~48% more postings than pg_search on `year` *and* returns the
correct English result set. Keep that in view when reading the common-term row.

## Where 1.5.9 landed

Confirmed on identical hardware and corpus:

| | 1.5.8 | 1.5.9 | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| rare k10 | 10.2 | **5.85** | **1.74×** |
| mid k10 | 11.3 | 10.69 | 1.06× |
| 2-term OR | 6.17* | **4.32** | **1.43×** |

(*measured on this same host during the 1.5.9 A/B.) Both improvements hold at

## Honest reading

**vs pg_textsearch** (the like-for-like comparator — same PostgreSQL `english`
config, byte-identical match counts, same C-extension model):
pg_fts is **faster on rare** (5.85 vs 7.36), **slower on mid** (10.69 vs 7.96),
**2.7× slower on common k10**, **1.4× faster on common k100**, with a **25%
smaller index**, plus `count(*)`/AND/OR/phrase/prefix that pg_textsearch does not
offer at all. Roughly a wash on ranked latency with a real size win and a much
larger capability surface.

**vs pg_search and vchord**: both are 2-3× faster than pg_fts on rare/mid and
**~26× / ~16× faster on common k10**. This is the real remaining gap and it is
not explained away by the stemming difference (that only accounts for ~48% more
postings on `year`, not 26×). Both use their own posting formats rather than
PostgreSQL's analyzer, and vchord pays for it in size (2.0× ours), pg_search in
correctness (no stemming) and in `count(*)` (13.63 ms vs our 2.20 ms).

**Where we clearly win:** smallest index in the field; fastest exact
`count(*)` (6× faster than pg_search, the only other engine that can do it at
all); rare-term latency beats the like-for-like comparator; and the
boolean/NEAR/prefix/fuzzy/regex/phrase query language none of the other three
match. **Where we clearly lose:** common-term ranked top-k, and phrase
(90.7 vs 22.9 ms).

## The common-term gap is now the top perf item

Common k10 at 55.97 ms against pg_search's 2.12 ms is the single worst number in
the table, and it is *not* a doclen problem any more — 1.5.9 fixed that. Per
`NOTE_WAND_PRUNING_2026-09-04.md`, `year` scores 670,976 of 735k postings with
only 500 block-skips: the block-max bound is tight and the threshold healthy, but
they sit ~12% apart across a flat impact plateau, so WAND cannot prune. The
measured per-posting cost is now the posting scan itself. Closing this needs a
change of a different kind (impact-ordered or impact-quantized postings), which
`NOTE_IMPACT_ORDERING.md` shows conflicts with the docid ordering that
count/AND/phrase/prefix depend on. That trade-off is the next real design
question, not a micro-optimisation.

## Correctness gate
Ranked top-k **parity PASS on all 10 cases** — `slovakia`, `hungary`, `year`,
`slovakia & hungary`, `slovakia | hungary`, each at k=10 and k=100, against an
exact `fts_bm25` sort over the heap. Raw output in
`data_5way_159b/pgfts_parity.txt`.

## Caveats
- pg_textsearch match counts derived by index-scan exhaustion (it exposes no df
  function); the `year` case took 475 s, which is an artifact of that method and
  **not** a latency measurement.
- pg_search's phrase uses its `###` operator; plain `@@@` does not accept phrases.
- vchord is ranking-only: no count/AND/phrase/prefix to measure.
- `pg_prewarm` was unavailable on the pg_fts host; with `shared_buffers=64GB` and
  8 repeated runs the timings are stably warm, which the run-to-run spread
  confirms.
