# M4 + M1: the competitor set on TIN's rig (2026-10-29)

**Current.**  This places pg_fts on the axis of PlanetScale's TIN article ("Introducing TIN",
2026-09-16).  It does not compare with TIN, which cannot be obtained (`NOTE_VS_TIN_2026-09-14.md`).
TIN's published numbers are not restated or compared here (AGENTS rule 10).  Scripts, the query
trace and raw pgbench output are in `data_m4_2026-10-29/`.

| query trace | 3,762 synthetic queries: substrings of 2-15 words, each as AND / OR / phrase (not published) | **same recipe, our own draw**: 1,254 substrings x 3 = 3,762 (`m4prep.py`, seed 20261029; `queries.tsv` md5 1636eaa6fb11) |
| load generator | ParadeDB Benchmarker (k6), prewarmed | pgbench `-M extended` (query text bound as a parameter, as the Benchmarker sends it), on CPUs 8-31, 16 clients, 60 s warm-up, then 300 s (W1, W2) or 600 s (W3) |
| client count | not stated | 16 |

Each engine indexes the post body (`content`) in its own documented form:
- ParadeDB: `bm25 (id, content)`, default tokenizer.
- pg_textsearch: `bm25 (content)`, `text_config 'english'`.
- GIN: `to_tsvector('english', content)`.
- pg_fts: `fts (to_ftsdoc('english', content)) WITH (positions = on)`.

Query forms per engine are in `m4.sh` (`qsql`):
- **pg_fts** quotes each word, because bare `and`/`or`/`not` are ftsquery keyword operators.  The
  first pg_fts run (20:58) was invalid: unquoted, those words were syntax errors and pgbench
  aborted 16 of 16 clients.  Its output is kept as the `run1_invalid` lines in `m4.log`.
- **GIN** ranks with `ts_rank_cd`.

pg_textsearch supports only disjunctions, so W1 and W2 are n/a for it, as in the article.

## Reading it

Where pg_fts is ahead:
- **Conjunctions and phrases (W2): 2.4x ParadeDB's throughput**, with a p99 2.4x lower.
- It is also ahead per query in W1: AND 119 ms against ParadeDB's 169, phrase 128 against 545.

Where pg_fts is behind:
- **Long disjunctions (2-15 words OR'ed).**  Mean 1,555 ms against ParadeDB's 334, so W1 as a
  whole is 26.4 tps against 45.4.  ROADMAP A2 already records wide ORs as the weak band; this
  rig makes it much larger than the 4-term OR on Wikipedia showed.
- **Concurrent updates (W3, the M1 cross-engine write measurement).**  pg_fts completed 25
  UPDATEs/s of the 1,000/s target, and readers fell to 1.7 tps.  ParadeDB sustained 999/s with
  26.7 tps of readers; pg_textsearch, as the article found, stalls writers (1.7/s).
  - Each pg_fts UPDATE re-analyzes the post through the expression index and appends to the
    pending list; inline flushes run every 20,000 pending documents (`pg_fts.pending_flush_threshold`).
  - The index grew from 26 GB to 44 GB during the run.
  - Not diagnosed here (ROADMAP I21).
- **Build memory.**  pg_fts documents its build peak as `shared_buffers + (max_parallel_maintenance_workers + 1) x 2 x maintenance_work_mem`.
  That is 168 GB under these settings, against a 24 GB `maintenance_work_mem` the other engines
  stay within.  It was OOM-killed at 32 GB and at 64 GB, and peaked at 132-141 GB.  Core
  PostgreSQL's parallel builds divide `maintenance_work_mem` among participants; pg_fts gives
  each participant up to twice the whole budget (ROADMAP I22).

**Not measured:**
- the article's in-memory count workload (Wikipedia, 8 GB);
- a second run per engine.  Each number is one run, so no run-to-run spread is reported.  Hold
  them as indicative until reproduced (AGENTS rule 4).
